# ASH Captions v0.6 — design

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: drafted from Ghazi's list of seven and the
2026-09-05 competitor research; awaiting his go on the order.
Builds on `2026-09-04-v0.5-design.md`.

## Goal

v0.5.1 is what the six editors are installing. Their first week produced
one list, and the research produced one surprise:

**We are already at parity on animation.** Karaoke fill, per-word colour
highlight, pop on the spoken word, fade, slide, drop, box highlight,
one-word-at-a-time — every effect the paid tools ship and editors reach
for, we have, across 36 looks. Zubtitle sells two animations. What
Captions.ai calls "75+ styles" are bundles of font+colour+motion, not
separable effects.

What we do not have is everything **around** the captions:

- No way to fix a word. Every competitor has this; it is the gap that
  makes the tool look unfinished. Today the only two options are to add a
  glossary line and **re-run the whole job**, or to hand-edit the `.srt`
  in the output folder, which the next restyle or burn overwrites.
- No way to style one word differently from its neighbours.
- No download anywhere in the product. Every path to a finished file is
  "Open folder", which opens Explorer **on the machine running the app** —
  so an editor reaching the page over the LAN cannot get a file at all.
  `GET /api/jobs/{id}/files` exists and says in its own docstring that it
  is there "so the page can offer downloads". No page calls it.
- A 3-second "Render preview" on one page and a full "Burn this look" on
  another, both producing an MP4, neither named so you can tell which is
  the export. This is our own invention: no competitor ships a short
  render that can be mistaken for an export.
- A Styles page that silently changes every future job on the PC.

Six pieces:

1. Fix the words
2. Style one word
3. Export everywhere, named honestly
4. The Styles page tells the truth
5. The reel look
6. Release 0.6.0

Out of scope, on a later spec: automatic keyword emphasis, emoji, and
landscape-to-9:16 reframe (v0.7).

## What was measured before writing this

Nothing below rests on what the ASS format is documented to support. Each
was burned with the bundled ffmpeg and measured on the pixels:

- **Animated scale, position, fade and rotation all render.** A word taken
  40% → 115% → 100% measured 112x42 → 210x78 → 197x74 px; a word given
  `\move` travelled 107 px in 380 ms; `\fad` and `\frz` both took effect.
- **`\blur` renders and animates.** `\blur16` decaying to `\blur0` over
  500 ms took soft-edge pixels from 11412 down to 413, settling exactly on
  time. An earlier measurement said otherwise; it was blurring a black
  outline against a black background and was wrong. `\be` renders but
  ignores `\t`, so blur animation must use `\blur`.
- **Per-word overrides compose with the active-word pop.** One line
  carrying four treatments — white bold, amber bold at 125%, white italic,
  mint at 140% with a pop transform — rendered correctly in one event.
  This is the mechanism section 2 depends on.
- **Free per-word placement reproduces the reference layout.** Four words
  at their own positions, sizes, colours and arrival times, one event each,
  render as a close match to the frame from Ghazi's reel. This is what
  makes v0.7 a renderer change rather than a new pipeline.

The scripts are in the session scratchpad (`anim_spike.py`,
`perword_spike.py`); they are spikes, not tests, and are not in the repo.

## 1. Fix the words

### Behaviour

The Studio's transcript panel becomes editable. Click a word, type, press
Enter. Two commit actions sit side by side in the same popup, Descript's
pattern:

- **Fix this one** — changes this occurrence.
- **Fix every "haramienta"** — changes every occurrence in this job, with
  the count shown so nobody is surprised.

A third action, **Always spell it this way**, adds the correction to the
job's client glossary so the next job gets it right at transcription time.
This is the pattern Submagic sells as a custom dictionary; we already have
the glossary machinery and have simply never offered it from the place
where the editor notices the problem.

Also on a word: **Split line here** (Enter at the caret) and **Merge with
next line**. A line longer than the look's `max_words` shows a quiet
warning rather than silently overflowing — VEED surfaces line length as an
actionable warning and it is the one piece of their editing UI worth
copying outright.

Timing: dragging a word's left or right edge in the transcript row moves
its start or end, clamped to its neighbours, minimum 60 ms. One word at a
time, Descript's "wordbar" behaviour. No timeline; the transcript row is
the timeline.

Every edit re-renders the `.ass` through the existing restyle path (about
230 ms) and rewrites the `.srt` and `.txt`. Nothing re-transcribes.

### Data

The transcript record (`<stem>.transcript.json`) is the source of truth and
gains, per word: `"edited": true` when its text was changed by hand, and
`"retimed": true` when its start or end was dragged. The record gains a
top-level `"revision": <int>`, incremented on every edit, so the Studio can
detect that another tab has changed it underneath.

Edits mutate the word list in place. Splitting a line inserts a break
marker; merging removes one. Line boundaries are today decided entirely by
`engine/rules.py:build_cards`, from `style.layout.max_words`, a `min_words`
floor and `settings.silence_gap_seconds` — all global or style-level, with
no per-job override anywhere. So `build_cards` gains one parameter: a set
of word indexes that must start a new card, and a set that must not. That
is the whole mechanism; everything else about card building is unchanged,
and a transcript with no markers builds byte-identical cards.

The confidence values (`p`) of an edited word
are dropped, not kept — a hand-typed word has no model confidence, and
showing the old number would be a lie. The caption check counts it as
resolved.

`en_words` is left alone by a source-text edit and marked stale; the
"Translate to check" button re-runs the English pass from the edited
transcript.

### API

- `PATCH /api/jobs/{id}/transcript` — body is a list of operations:
  `{"op": "set_text", "index": 12, "text": "herramienta", "all": false}`,
  `{"op": "retime", "index": 12, "start": 10.82, "end": 11.66}`,
  `{"op": "split", "index": 12}`, `{"op": "merge", "index": 12}`.
  Returns the new revision and the re-rendered caption count. Rejects a
  stale `revision`, so two tabs cannot silently clobber each other — the
  same class of bug that bit two-tab restyling in v0.5.
- `POST /api/jobs/{id}/glossary` — `{"from": "haramienta", "to": "herramienta"}`,
  writes to the job's client glossary (shared glossary when the job has no
  client), validated at the boundary like every other glossary write.

### Tests

Operations are pure functions over a transcript record, tested without a
server: set_text with and without `all`, retime clamped by neighbours,
split, merge, revision conflict, confidence dropped on edit, `en_words`
marked stale. Then a route test per operation, and one live check: edit a
word in the real Studio, confirm the `.ass`, `.srt` and `.txt` on disk all
carry it and the video still plays.

## 2. Style one word

### Behaviour

Clicking a word — on the video or in the transcript — selects it and opens
a small toolbar: colour, size (percentage of the look's size), bold,
italic. Every control shows what the look already gives the word, so the
toolbar is a diff, not a blank form.

Above the controls, one line naming the blast radius, Riverside's pattern
of making scope an explicit choice at the moment of editing rather than a
setting on another page:

> Changing **this word** only. Change the look instead →

A word carrying an override shows a small dot in the transcript, CapCut's
"you have detached this caption" indicator made visible before the
confusion rather than after it. **Reset word** and **Reset all overrides
on this job** undo it.

Deliberately **not** per-word font family and not per-word outline.
Kapwing draws the line in exactly this place — per-word bold, italic and
colour, everything else global — and their reason holds for us: the
combinatorial surface is where this feature turns into a mess. Font and
outline stay a property of the look.

Per-letter styling falls out of the same mechanism (a selection is a run of
characters, not necessarily a whole word) and the renderer handles it, but
v0.6 ships word granularity only; letters are a v0.7 question alongside
free placement.

### Data

Overrides live on the word in the transcript record, not in `JobOptions`:

```json
{"w": "herramienta", "s": 10.82, "e": 11.66, "p": 0.988,
 "style": {"colour": "#FFD166", "scale": 1.25, "bold": true}}
```

Indexed by the word itself, so a split or merge carries the override with
the text instead of shifting an index array out of alignment. All keys
optional; an empty or absent `style` is the look's own rendering, byte for
byte, so every existing golden test stays valid.

### Engine

`styles/render.py:_line_text` already wraps each word in its own `{...}`
override block — that is the whole seam. The per-word override is merged
into the block that is already emitted:

- `colour` → `\c` (overrides both the base and active colour for that word)
- `scale` → `\fscx`/`\fscy`, composed with the active-word pop rather than
  replacing it
- `bold` → `\b1`, `italic` → `\i1`, each closed after the word

Order matters: the look's tags are emitted first and the word's override
last, so the override wins. `render_ass` output is unchanged for any
transcript without overrides.

### API

Overrides ride the transcript PATCH as a fifth operation,
`{"op": "set_style", "index": 12, "style": {...} | null}`, so one endpoint
owns the transcript and one revision counter guards it.

### Tests

Golden: a transcript with no overrides renders byte-identical `.ass` to
today. Then one test per property, one for composition with the active-word
pop, one for the override surviving a split, and a real-libass pixel test
that a word given `#FFD166` is actually drawn in that colour.

## 3. Export everywhere, named honestly

### Behaviour

**The word "export" appears exactly once per surface and always means the
finished file.** VEED's model: one action, and everything else is
explicitly a preview.

An **Export** control appears in the Studio header, on every finished queue
row, and on the Styles page. It offers, with sizes:

- Video with captions burned in (`.captioned.mp4`) — queues the burn when
  the job has not been burned yet, downloads it when it has
- Subtitles (`.srt`)
- Styled subtitles (`.ass`)
- Transcript (`.txt`)
- English subtitles (`.en.srt`), when the job has them

These are real browser downloads, so an editor on another machine can
finally get their files. "Open folder" and "Copy path" stay for the person
sitting at the machine.

The Styles page's **Render preview** becomes **Preview 3 seconds**, with
the duration in the button rather than in a sentence above it, and its
result panel says "This is a 3-second preview. To export the whole video,
open it in the Studio." That single sentence is the actual fix for the
reported bug.

While a burn runs, the Export control shows progress and the page says the
work continues if you navigate away — Kapwing's "you can walk away"
affordance, which our queue already supports and never advertised.

### API

- `GET /api/jobs/{id}/files` — already exists, already lists names and
  sizes, and is finally called by a page.
- `GET /api/jobs/{id}/files/{name}` — new; serves one named output file as
  an attachment. The name is matched against the listing rather than joined
  onto a path, so nothing outside the job's output directory is reachable.
  Same `X-ASH-Client` and Origin checks as every other route.

### Tests

Route tests for the listing and the download, including a traversal attempt
(`..`, an absolute path, a name not in the listing) returning 404 rather
than a file. A live check: download each file type from a real browser and
confirm the bytes match the file on disk.

## 4. The Styles page tells the truth

### Behaviour

Three changes, all about scope and visibility:

**It says what it changes.** A saved style is written to the user styles
directory and layered over the shipped one **by name**
(`styles/library.py:85-89`), and a job stores only the style's *name*, never
its content (`pipeline/db.py:62`). So editing GLOW MINT changes what every
job using GLOW MINT renders as — including old ones, the moment anybody
restyles or re-burns them. Only the files already written to disk are safe.
The warning says exactly that, next to the Save button:

> Saving changes GLOW MINT for every job that uses it on this PC, including
> old jobs if they are restyled or burned again. Files already produced
> keep the captions they have.

Save-as is offered beside it as the non-destructive path. A look with a
local override says so and offers **Reset to built-in**, which already
works and is already there.

**The look cards move.** Today they are static pictures, so an editor picks
a motion effect having never seen motion — the single strangest thing in
the product. Each card becomes a small JASSUB canvas looping two seconds of
the look's own animation, using the renderer that already runs in the
Studio. The looks list, the Styles page cards, and the Studio's list all
share one component.

**The Motion tab stops hiding what the format supports.** The schema has
had `exit` and `duration_ms` on both transitions since v0.3 and the UI
shows neither. Both appear: an Exit row matching the Entrance row, and a
speed control for each, which is the control surface from the reference
Ghazi sent (Animation In, Animation Out, and a speed for each).

### Tests

A page test that the warning text exists and names the look; a node test of
the shared preview component; a schema round-trip proving exit and duration
survive the editor.

## 5. The reel look

The five references Ghazi sent were downloaded and stepped through at 40 ms
per frame. Two of them (the real-estate reels) are the brief; one is a VFX
edit whose captions are plain; one is a Final Cut tutorial showing the
control panel he wants; one has no captions at all.

The two that matter do three things, and only three:

1. **Several treatments inside one caption.** "Westbrook's" small white
   italic, "2nd" huge yellow, "Highest" large black, "residential" small
   italic — one phrase, four sizes, four colours, two fonts.
2. **Words placed freely and kept.** Each word lands at its own spot, at
   its own moment, and stays while the next arrives.
3. **Words behind the speaker.** We already do this, and it is what makes
   them look expensive.

Measured entrances, from their own frames:

- **Stretch-collapse**: the word enters about 180% wide and snaps to 100%
  over ~120 ms while fading in. `\fscx180\t(0,120,\fscx100)` + `\fad`.
- **Fade-settle**: fades in over ~240 ms while shrinking from ~110% and
  dropping a few pixels. `\fad` + `\t(\fscx\fscy)` + `\move`.

### The decision that makes this shippable

Nobody will hand-place seven hundred words. **The look lays the words out;
the editor adjusts what it got wrong.** A free-placement look is a set of
rules — how many words are on screen at once, which get the big treatment,
where each lands, how far they may stray from the column — and the renderer
assigns each word a slot from its length and its part in the phrase (a
short connector like "the", "on a", "and" gets the small italic treatment;
the long content word gets the large one). The editor then drags any word
that landed badly, using the same handle that already moves the caption.

### Data

`Layout` gains `mode: "line" | "free"` (default `"line"`, so all 36 shipped
looks are unchanged) and, for free mode, a `slots` list giving each slot a
position, a size multiplier, a colour role and an italic flag. A word may
override its own position exactly as it overrides its own colour, in the
same `style` key from section 2, with `x` and `y` as fractions of the frame.

### Engine

A fifth event builder beside `_standard_events`, `_box_events` and
`_karaoke_events`: `_free_events` emits **one event per word**, each with
its own `\pos`, `\fscx/\fscy`, `\c`, `\fn` and entrance, ending when the
phrase ends rather than when the next word starts — that is what makes
words accumulate instead of replace. Everything else in `render_ass` is
untouched, and `_card_events` gains one branch.

This was spiked before writing it down: four words at their own positions,
sizes, colours and arrival times rendered a close match to the reference
frame, in one `.ass`, with the bundled ffmpeg.

### UI

The looks list gains free-placement looks alongside the rest — they are
just looks, so the Studio needs no new page. Selecting a word already opens
the toolbar from section 2; in a free-placement look it also offers
**Move this word**, which turns the existing drag handle on that one word.

Three shipped to start: one modelled on the real-estate reels, one calmer
two-tier version, one big-number treatment for prices and stats.

### Tests

Golden files for the three new looks; a test that `mode: "line"` renders
byte-identical `.ass` to today; slot assignment as a pure function over a
card (short connector versus content word); a real-libass pixel test that
two words in one phrase are drawn at different sizes in different places.

## Work split

Five tracks, one worktree each, non-overlapping files, each with its own
port and data root. Merge order riskiest-shared-file first, as in v0.5.

| Track | Owns | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| A — transcript edits | `routes_transcript.py`, `app/transcript.py`, new `web/static/studio_edit.js`/`.css` | — |
| B — per-word style | `styles/render.py` (`_line_text` only), new `web/static/studio_word.js`/`.css` | A's PATCH shape (agreed up front, not the code) |
| C — export | `routes_review.py`, new `web/static/export.js`/`.css`, `queue.js` and `studio.html` hooks | — |
| D — Styles page | `style_editor.html`/`.js`/`.css`, new shared look-card component | — |
| F — the reel look | `styles/schema.py` (Layout), new `styles/render_free.py`, three new `styles/*.json` | B's per-word `style` key |
| E — guide, screenshots, release | `docs/`, `scripts/guide_screenshots.py` | all |

A and B both touch the transcript record: A owns the file format and
publishes the word-entry shape; B adds one optional key to it. B owns
`styles/render.py`; F owns a new `render_free.py` and the one `_card_events`
branch that reaches it, so the two never edit the same function. A does not
touch `styles/` at all.

Guide gains, on top of section 6's list: "The reel look".

The live-app checks and the screenshots belong to the integrator after the
merge, as in v0.5 — they need every track present, and they are where the
real bugs surface.
